# Gray Wolf (*Canis lupus*)

The gray wolf is the closest wild relative of the domestic dog: wolves are the
dog's ancestor and share nearly the whole body plan.

## Physical characteristics

Wolves are large canids: 30–80 kg, shoulder height 66–81 cm, with a powerful,
lean build adapted for long-distance pursuit. Coat is a dense double layer,
usually grizzled gray with agouti banding, sometimes white, black or brown
phases. Males are noticeably larger than females.

## Body shape

Long-legged and deep-chested with a level topline. The chest is narrower
relative to body width than in many bulldog-type breeds, giving a racy,
endurance-oriented silhouette. Overall proportions are more uniform than the
extreme breed variety seen in domestic dogs.

## Facial features

Broad skull with a long, heavy muzzle; the ears are erect and triangular but relatively
short, with rounded tips, and set wide apart. The nose bridge is straight with little or no stop, so the
forehead flows continuously into the muzzle. Eyes are amber or yellow, giving a
characteristic intense expression. Muzzle width and head mass are typically
greater than in most dogs of the same size.

## Legs and paws

Long straight limbs with large paws that act as snowshoes — paw size often
looks oversized relative to the leg. Non-retractable claws; the track shows a
large heel pad with two leading lobes. Legs are proportionally longer than those
of most short-legged domestic breeds.

## Tail

Bushy, held straight or in a low sickle when relaxed, rarely curled over the
back. The tail is used for signaling and is typically carried lower than a
spitz dog's curled tail.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Wolves and dogs can look nearly identical, especially in shepherd, husky and
malamute-type breeds. Skull and dental morphology overlap, and dogs and wolves
interbreed. A wolf-like photograph alone does not make an animal a domestic dog.

## Differences from domestic dogs

Wolves have a longer muzzle with no stop, smaller and more rounded ears,
disproportionately large paws, amber eyes, and a straighter tail carriage.
Wolves also lack the breed-typical features of domestic dogs (floppy ears,
curled tails, brachycephalic faces).
