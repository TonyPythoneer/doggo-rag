# Dingo (*Canis lupus dingo*)

The dingo is a wild dog native to Australia that can interbreed freely with
domestic dogs.

## Physical characteristics

Dingoes weigh 15–20 kg with a shoulder height of 40–65 cm. The coat is
typically sandy, ginger, gold or tawny, sometimes with white markings on the
chest, muzzle, paws or tail tip. Black-and-tan, sable and all-white coats also occur. The fur is dense but lacks the heavy undercoat
of northern domestic breeds.

## Body shape

Lean, wedge-shaped and well-proportioned: longer than it is tall, with a
level topline and a deep but narrow chest. The build is more uniform and
graceful than most domestic dogs, without the exaggerated proportions of
established breeds.

## Facial features

Broad skull with a pointed muzzle and a distinct stop-less slope. Ears are
large, triangular, fully erect and set wide apart on the head. Eyes are
almond-shaped and yellow-amber. The overall face is often described as
"fox-like but dog-sized."

## Legs and paws

Long, lean legs with large paws for their body size. The gait is famously
elastic and springy. Paw prints resemble a large domestic dog's but are more
oval, with the toes held closer together.

## Tail

Thick, bushy and carried in a low curve or straight out; it is never curled
over the back like a husky's. The tail tip is often white.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Dingoes look like medium-sized, short-coated tan domestic dogs and can
interbreed with them. A dog-like build alone does not make an animal a domestic
dog: the dingo is a wild canid, not a domestic dog.

## Differences from domestic dogs

Dingoes typically have a more uniform wild-type build, no breed-specific
features (floppy ears, curly tails, brachycephalic faces), a distinctive
springy gait and usually a white-tipped bushy tail.
