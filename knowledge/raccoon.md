# Raccoon (*Procyon lotor*)

Raccoons are medium-sized nocturnal mammals of North America with a black
face mask, a ringed tail and dexterous front paws.

## Physical characteristics

Raccoons weigh 5–26 kg with a body length of 40–70 cm. The fur is grizzled
gray-brown over a dense undercoat. The body is stocky and rounded, with a
distinctly rotund appearance when well fed.

## Body shape

Low-slung, compact and pot-bellied with relatively short legs. The posture is
slightly hunched, unlike the upright, leggy stance of a dog.

## Facial features

A black "bandit mask" across the eyes bordered by white fur is diagnostic. The
muzzle is short, pointed and black-tipped, flanked by white whisker patches.
Ears are small, rounded and edged in white. The face reads as cat-like rather
than canid.

## Legs and paws

Long dexterous front paws with five spreadable toes that can manipulate objects
— almost hand-like. Raccoons walk plantigrade (on the soles), so their tracks
show a hand-like print unlike a dog's compact four-toe pad track.

## Tail

Bushy with 5–7 alternating dark and light rings, usually carried low and
horizontally; it can be ringed in ways that superficially resemble a fox's
brush but the banding is unmistakable.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Raccoons are about the size of small to medium dogs, are sometimes found in
urban settings, and can be gray-brown like many dogs. They are quadrupeds with
similar gait in distant photographs.

## Differences from domestic dogs

The black eye mask, hand-like five-toed paws, plantigrade stance, short pointed
muzzle and ringed tail separate raccoons from dogs.
