# Domestic Dog (*Canis lupus familiaris*)

The domestic dog is a domesticated canid descended from the gray wolf, found
worldwide as a companion, working and livestock-guarding animal.

## Physical characteristics

Dogs are extraordinarily varied in size, coat and build, from 2 kg toy breeds to
over 90 kg working breeds. Common coat types are short, double, wire and long;
colors range over the whole mammalian palette. Adult weight spans roughly
1–90 kg, with shoulder height 15–80 cm. Domestic dogs show far more morphological
variation than any wild canid.

## Body shape

Build varies by breed but generally shows a deep chest, well-spaced forelimbs,
and a proportionally shorter muzzle than a wolf. Legs are usually straight and
medium in length relative to the torso; the body is often slightly longer than
it is tall in many common breeds.

## Facial features

The muzzle is typically broad and blunt compared with foxes and coyotes. Ears
are extremely variable: erect, semi-prick, folded, floppy or cropped. The stop
(brow transition between forehead and muzzle) is usually pronounced. Eyes are
forward-facing with a wide range of colors. The skull is generally rounded with
a shorter rostrum than wild canids.

## Legs and paws

Paws are compact, with non-retractable claws; toe alignment is usually neat and
parallel. Limb length relative to body size varies enormously by breed, but
domestic dogs commonly show heavier, thicker legs than coyotes or foxes, with
less elongated metapodials.

## Tail

Tail shape varies widely: curled over the back (spitz types), sickle-shaped,
straight, or naturally bobbed. Most dogs carry the tail lower than a wolf's
relaxed horizontal carriage; many breeds have a naturally high tail set.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Wolves, coyotes and dingoes share the dog's body plan: digitigrade stance,
non-retractable claws and comparable ear and muzzle shape. A dog-shaped body
alone does not show that an animal is a domestic dog.

## Differences from domestic dogs

Features seen in domestic dogs but not in wild canids include floppy or folded
ears, a tail curled over the back, a short flat face, very short or very long
legs for the body size, and coat patterns such as merle, brindle or large patches. Wolves are typically
larger with a broader head, longer muzzle and larger paws; foxes are far smaller
and more slender.
