# Hyena (Hyaenidae)

Spotted hyenas are large African carnivores that resemble dogs at a
glance but are not canids.

## Physical characteristics

Spotted hyenas weigh 45–82 kg with a shoulder height of 70–91 cm — larger than
most dogs. The coat is sandy-yellow to gray-brown with round dark spots, and a
coarse mane runs along the neck and back.

## Body shape

Unmistakable sloping profile: the forequarters are markedly taller and heavier
than the hindquarters, giving a downward-sloping back. The neck is thick and
muscular with a heavy head carried low. No domestic dog matches this sloped
build exactly, though some bulldog mixes hint at it.

## Facial features

Broad, rounded muzzle (shorter and wider than a wolf's), rounded ears set low
and wide on a massive skull, and dark brown eyes. The face is blunt and
powerful, with a strong jaw line; there is little or no stop.

## Legs and paws

Front legs longer and thicker than the hind legs — the defining proportion.
Paws are large with blunt non-retractable claws; the hind tracks are notably
smaller than the front tracks.

## Tail

Medium-length, coarse-haired, carried hanging down; it is not bushy like a
fox's and never curled over the back. The tail darkens toward the tip.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Hyenas share the canid-like body plan at a glance: digitigrade locomotion,
four-toe paw prints with claws, a dog-like muzzle and similar coloration.
Their dog-like appearance is why they are often mistaken for domestic dogs.

## Differences from domestic dogs

The sloping back with longer front legs, massive blunt head with rounded low
ears, spotted coat and hanging coarse tail distinguish hyenas. Hyenas are
more closely related to cats and mongoose than to dogs.
