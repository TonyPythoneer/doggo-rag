# Koala (*Phascolarctos cinereus*)

Koalas are arboreal marsupials of Australia and with no canid features: a stocky, tailless body, round fuzzy ears and a large
spoon-shaped nose.

## Physical characteristics

Koalas weigh 4–15 kg depending on region, with a body length of 60–85 cm. The
fur is thick and woolly, gray to brown-gray, with white patches on the chest
and rump. Males have a scent gland on the chest.

## Body shape

Stocky, rounded body with almost no visible neck and famously short limbs
proportioned for gripping branches. The overall silhouette is a compact,
rounded blob quite unlike the leggy frame of any dog.

## Facial features

Large, rounded, furry ears with prominent white tufts; a very large, dark,
leathery, spoon-shaped nose; small dark eyes set wide apart. The face has no
muzzle in the canid sense — the nose dominates the head.

## Legs and paws

Forepaws with two opposable thumbs (five digits total) adapted for climbing,
and rough padded soles. Hind feet have a grooming claw and fused second and
third toes. The paw structure is entirely unlike a dog's compact paw.

## Tail

Koalas have no visible tail. The absence of a tail alone distinguishes a koala
from any dog, fox or wolf in a photograph.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Both are furry, four-limbed mammals that can appear gray-brown, and both can
sit upright. In very poor lighting or heavy blur, a rounded gray animal might
be mistaken for a small dog.

## Differences from domestic dogs

No tail, huge rounded ears, a giant leathery nose, thumb-like gripping paws
and a round body shape immediately distinguish koalas. Koalas are marsupials,
not canids.
