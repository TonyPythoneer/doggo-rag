# Coyote (*Canis latrans*)

Coyotes are medium-sized North American canids that overlap heavily with
dog-like appearance and are often mistaken for domestic dogs.

## Physical characteristics

Coyotes weigh 8–20 kg with a shoulder height of 58–68 cm — the size of a small
to medium dog such as a border collie. Coat is tan, grayish or cinnamon with
grizzled banding, paler on the belly and throat, often with a reddish tint
behind the ears.

## Body shape

Slender and narrow-chested, with long thin legs and a compact, athletic frame.
The build is lighter than a wolf's and more leggy than a typical pet dog of
the same weight, giving a distinctly "leggy" silhouette.

## Facial features

Narrow, pointed muzzle — longer and thinner than most dogs' but blunter than a
fox's. Ears are large, triangular and held upright. Eyes are amber-yellow. The
forehead is broad with a slight stop. The overall expression is fox-like but
scaled up to dog size.

## Legs and paws

Long, thin limbs with relatively small, narrow paws for their height. Tracks
are more elongated and neatly oval than a domestic dog's, with claw marks that
are finer and closer together.

## Tail

Thick and bushy, usually carried straight back or drooping while walking, with
a black-tipped tail. It is never curled over the back.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Coyotes are true canids with the same gait, paw structure and dentition as
dogs. Stray and pariah-type dogs in North America are frequently
misidentified as coyotes; the two also hybridize in the wild.

## Differences from domestic dogs

Coyotes have a narrower chest, longer and thinner legs, a more pointed muzzle,
and a black-tipped tail held low. Size is usually smaller than most medium
dogs.
