# Red Fox (*Vulpes vulpes*)

The red fox is a small canid frequently misread as a small or juvenile dog.

## Physical characteristics

Red foxes are light and slender: 3–14 kg, body length 45–90 cm, shoulder height
35–50 cm — roughly the size of a medium cat rather than a dog. The classic coat
is bright rust-red above, white below, with black markings on the ears, lower
legs and muzzle edges.

## Body shape

Narrow, low-slung and cat-like: short legs relative to body length, a light
frame and a low shoulder. The silhouette is far more delicate than any but the
smallest toy dogs, with a noticeably thin neck.

## Facial features

Very large, pointed ears set high and often edged in black; a sharply pointed,
narrow muzzle with a black nose and white cheeks. Eyes are amber with a
vertical-slit pupil appearance in bright light. The face reads as wedge-shaped
rather than blunt.

## Legs and paws

Short, slender legs with relatively small paws; the black "stockings" on the
lower legs are distinctive. Paws are compact and the stance is delicate,
lacking the heavy bone of a dog.

## Tail

A very bushy, thick "brush," almost as long as the body, usually tipped in
white. Foxes carry the tail low while moving; it is not curled over the back.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Foxes are canids with digitigrade locomotion, non-retractable claws, similar
paw prints and canine dentition. Red-colored domestic dogs and small juvenile
dogs can be confused with foxes at a glance, especially in motion blur.

## Differences from domestic dogs

The fox's pointed muzzle, huge ears, black stockings, white tail tip and
cat-sized, low-slung body distinguish it from any domestic dog. Foxes cannot
bark or howl like dogs.
