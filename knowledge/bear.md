# Bear (Ursidae)

Bears are large mammals whose bulk can be misread for a large dog in cropped or
distant photographs.

## Physical characteristics

Depending on species, bears weigh 60–600+ kg, with body lengths of 1–3 m. Coats
range from white and brown to black, dense and shaggy. The brown bear and
grizzly are most likely to appear in general wildlife imagery.

## Body shape

Massive, heavy-bodied and muscular with a prominent shoulder hump (brown
bears) and a large rump. The frame is far heavier than any domestic dog,
though a cropped photo of a large dark dog can be misleading.

## Facial features

Broad, dish-shaped or convex profile with a long muzzle, small rounded ears
set wide apart on a wide head, and small eyes. The head is large relative to
the neck, with no visible stop in most species.

## Legs and paws

Enormous, pillar-like limbs with huge non-retractile claws — plantigrade
locomotion, so the whole sole touches the ground. Tracks are enormous
hand-like prints with clear claw marks, far larger than any dog's.

## Tail

Bears have only a very short, stubby tail (7–13 cm) that is usually hidden in
the fur — effectively absent in a photograph.

## Similarities to domestic dogs

Both are furry quadrupeds with non-retractile claws and similar coat color
ranges. Long-haired large dogs (Newfoundland, sheepdog) can be mistaken for
bear cubs at a distance.

## Differences from domestic dogs

Pillar legs, plantigrade gait, a shoulder hump, wide dish-faced head, small
round ears and a nearly invisible tail distinguish bears.
